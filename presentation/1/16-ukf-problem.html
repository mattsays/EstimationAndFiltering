UKF Failure: When Roots Go Negative
From a small \(\alpha\) to \(\sqrt{\text{negative}}\) in \(\mathbf{L}_{k+1}\), needed to predict step \(k+2\)
1. Small \(\alpha\) ⇒ huge weights
With \(\varepsilon:=n+\lambda=\alpha^2(n+\kappa)\to 0\):
\(\displaystyle w_i=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\to+\infty,\quad w_0^c\approx-\frac{n}{\varepsilon}=-M\)
e.g. \(n=2,\ \kappa=0,\ \alpha=10^{-3}\Rightarrow M\approx10^{6}\)
→
2. Nonlinear \(\mathbf{f}\) breaks symmetry
The images \(\mathbf{y}_i=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_i)\) are no longer symmetric around \(\mathbf{y}_0\):
\(\displaystyle \mathbf{e}_0=\mathbf{y}_0-\bar{\mathbf{y}}\neq\mathbf{0}\)
(a linear \(\mathbf{f}\) gives \(\mathbf{e}_0=\mathbf{0}\): no damage)
→
3. Recombine into \(\mathbf{P}_{k+1}\)
\(\displaystyle \mathbf{P}_{k+1}=\color{red}{w_0^c}\,\mathbf{e}_0\mathbf{e}_0^T+\sum_{i=1}^{2n}w_i\,\mathbf{e}_i\mathbf{e}_i^T\)
with \(\mathbf{e}_i=\mathbf{y}_i-\bar{\mathbf{y}}\). The red term is subtracted: positive semidefiniteness is no longer guaranteed.
↓
4. \(\mathbf{P}_{k+1}\) is not PD
A \(2\times2\) covariance is PD only if both hold:
\(\displaystyle p_{11}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\det(\mathbf{P}_{k+1})>0\)
Every entry of \(\mathbf{P}_{k+1}\) is a sum in which one term carries the negative weight \(w_0^c\).
It depends on \(\alpha,\beta\) and on how strongly \(\mathbf{f}\) bends.
→
5. Cholesky for step \(k+2\)
We need \(\mathbf{L}_{k+1}\) with \(\mathbf{P}_{k+1}=\mathbf{L}_{k+1}\mathbf{L}_{k+1}^T\):
\(\displaystyle L_{11}=\sqrt{p_{11}}\quad(\text{fails if }p_{11}<0)\)
\(\displaystyle L_{22}=\sqrt{\det(\mathbf{P}_{k+1})/p_{11}}\quad(\text{fails if }\det<0)\)
→
6. Sigma points are NaN
\(\displaystyle \mathbf{x}_i=\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}\pm\gamma(\mathbf{L}_{k+1})_i=\text{NaN}\)
The prediction to \(k+2\) cannot start, and every later estimate is NaN.
Where and when: the damage is done in step \(k \to k+1\) (blocks 1 to 4), but nothing fails there. The crash comes one iteration later, when \(\mathbf{L}_{k+1}\) is needed to place the sigma points that predict \(k+2\).
