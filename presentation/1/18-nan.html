From Tug of War to NaN
A worked example, and why the next Cholesky takes \(\sqrt{\text{negative}}\)
Worked example (scalar): can the net really be negative?
\(n=1,\ \kappa=0,\ \alpha=0.1,\ f(x)=x^2,\ x\sim\mathcal{N}(0,P_k)\) ⇒ \(\varepsilon=0.01,\ \gamma=0.1,\ w_i=50,\ w_0=-99,\ w_0^c=-98.01+\beta\)
Points \(x_0=0,\ x_{1,2}=\pm\gamma\sqrt{P_k}\) ⇒ \(y_0=0,\ y_{1,2}=0.01P_k\) ⇒ \(\bar{y}=2\cdot50\cdot0.01P_k=P_k\) ⇒ \(e_0=-P_k,\ e_{1,2}=-0.99P_k\)
\(\beta=+2\) (in units of \(P_k^2\))
outer: \(\sum_{i=1}^{2}w_i e_i^2\)
+98.01
center: \(w_0^c e_0^2\)
−96.01
\(P_{k+1}=+2.00\,P_k^2>0\) ✓
\(\beta=-2\) (in units of \(P_k^2\))
outer: \(\sum_{i=1}^{2}w_i e_i^2\)
+98.01
center: \(w_0^c e_0^2\)
−100.01
\(P_{k+1}=-2.00\,P_k^2<0\) ✗
Here \(P_{k+1}=\beta P_k^2\) exactly. The true variance is \(\mathrm{Var}[x^2]=2P_k^2\): \(\beta=2\) recovers it, \(\beta=-2\) gives a negative variance.
4. When is a \(2\times2\) \(\mathbf{P}\) PD?
Sylvester's criterion:
\(\displaystyle \mathbf{P}\succ0\iff\begin{cases}p_{11}>0\\ \det\mathbf{P}>0\end{cases}\)
with \(\det\mathbf{P}=p_{11}p_{22}-p_{12}^2\). A direction with \(\mathbf{v}^T\mathbf{P}\mathbf{v}<0\) breaks one of the two.
→
5. Cholesky for step \(k+2\): \(\mathbf{P}_{k+1}=\mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^T\)
\(\displaystyle \begin{bmatrix}p_{11}&p_{12}\\p_{12}&p_{22}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}L_{11}^2&L_{11}L_{21}\\L_{11}L_{21}&L_{21}^2+L_{22}^2\end{bmatrix}\)
\(\displaystyle L_{11}=\sqrt{p_{11}},\qquad L_{21}=\frac{p_{12}}{L_{11}}\)
\(\displaystyle L_{22}=\sqrt{p_{22}-L_{21}^2}=\sqrt{\det\mathbf{P}/p_{11}}\)
\(\sqrt{\text{negative}}\) if \(p_{11}<0\) or \(\det\mathbf{P}<0\).
→
6. Sigma points are NaN
\(\displaystyle \mathbf{x}_i=\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}\pm\gamma(\mathbf{L}_{k+1})_i=\text{NaN}\)
No prediction for \(k+2\), and every later estimate is NaN.
Where and when: the damage is done in \(k\to k+1\) (\(\mathbf{P}_{k+1}\) is not PD), but nothing fails there. The crash comes one iteration later, at the Cholesky needed to place the sigma points for \(k+2\).
