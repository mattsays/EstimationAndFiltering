Why Exactly \(2n+1\) Symmetric Points?

Preserving Gaussian zero-skewness & extracting 2nd-order non-linear curvature

1. Why Symmetric Pairs \(\pm\mathbf{\delta}\)?

Gaussians are perfectly symmetric (\(\text{Skewness} = 0\)). Asymmetric point sets would introduce artificial skew. Symmetric pairs guarantee all odd moments cancel out:

\(\displaystyle (+\mathbf{\delta})^3 + (-\mathbf{\delta})^3 = 0 \implies \text{Zero artificial skew}\)

2. Aren't 2 Symmetrical Points Redundant? No!

Though inputs are symmetric; passing them through non-linear \(f_k(\mathbf{x})\) yields asymmetric outputs (\(\Delta y_+ \neq \Delta y_-\)). This measures local space bending!

\(\displaystyle \Delta y_+ \neq \Delta y_- \implies \text{Captures 2nd-order Taylor curvature } (\tfrac{1}{2}f''\mathbf{\delta}^2)\)
