Preserving Gaussian zero-skewness & extracting 2nd-order non-linear curvature

A. Mean + mirror pairs, equal weights (\(\mathbf{a}=\gamma\mathbf{L}_1,\ \mathbf{b}=\gamma\mathbf{L}_2\))
\(\displaystyle \sum_i \boldsymbol{\delta}_i=\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{0}\)
\(\displaystyle \sum_i \boldsymbol{\delta}_i^{\otimes 3}=\mathbf{a}^{\otimes 3}-\mathbf{a}^{\otimes 3}+\mathbf{b}^{\otimes 3}-\mathbf{b}^{\otimes 3}=\mathbf{0}\)
✓ each point has a mirror partner: odd moments cancel
B. Mean + triangle at 120°, equal weights (\(\boldsymbol{\delta}_k=r\,\mathbf{u}_k\), seen along \(x\))
\(\displaystyle \sum_k \delta_{k,x}=r\big[1-\tfrac12-\tfrac12\big]=0\)
\(\displaystyle \sum_k \delta_{k,x}^{3}=r^3\big[1^3+(-\tfrac12)^3+(-\tfrac12)^3\big]=\tfrac34\,r^3\neq0\)
✗ no mirror partners: the mean is fine, but a skew appears

2. Aren't 2 Symmetrical Points Redundant? No!

Though inputs are symmetric, passing them through a non-linear \(f(\mathbf{x})\) yields asymmetric outputs (\(\Delta y_+ \neq \Delta y_-\)). This measures local space bending!

\(\displaystyle \Delta y_+ \neq \Delta y_- \;\Rightarrow\; \text{curvature } \tfrac12 f''\delta^2\)
