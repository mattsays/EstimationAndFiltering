From Negative Variance to NaN

Why an invalid covariance crashes the filter one step later

Previous slide: in the scalar example \(P_{k+1}=p_{11}<0\), a negative variance.

4. When is \(\mathbf{P}\) positive-definite?

Sylvester's criterion for a \(2\times2\) matrix:

\(\displaystyle \mathbf{P}\succ0\iff\begin{cases}p_{11}>0\\ \det\mathbf{P}>0\end{cases}\)

In our scalar example \(P_{k+1}=p_{11}\): the first condition already fails.

→

5. Cholesky at step \(k+2\): \(\mathbf{P}_{k+1}=\mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^T\)

\(\displaystyle \begin{bmatrix}p_{11}&p_{12}\\p_{12}&p_{22}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}L_{11}^2&L_{11}L_{21}\\L_{11}L_{21}&L_{21}^2+L_{22}^2\end{bmatrix}\)
\(\displaystyle L_{11}=\sqrt{p_{11}},\qquad L_{21}=\frac{p_{12}}{L_{11}}\)
\(\displaystyle L_{22}=\sqrt{\det\mathbf{P}/p_{11}}\)

\(\sqrt{\text{negative}}\) as soon as \(p_{11}<0\) or \(\det\mathbf{P}<0\).

→

6. Sigma points are NaN

\(\displaystyle \mathbf{x}_i=\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}\pm\gamma(\mathbf{L})_i=\text{NaN}\)

No prediction for \(k+2\), and every later estimate is NaN.

Where and when: the damage is done in \(k\to k+1\) (\(\mathbf{P}_{k+1}\) is not PD), but nothing fails there. The crash comes one iteration later, at the Cholesky needed to place the sigma points for \(k+2\).
