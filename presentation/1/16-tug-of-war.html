From Tug of War to Negative Variance

A scalar example: can the UKF produce a negative variance?

Inputs

state \(x \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\, P_k)\)
function \(f(x) = x^2\)
parameters \(n=1,\ \kappa=0,\ \alpha=0.1\)
→

Sigma points

before \(f\) \(x_0=0,\ x_{1,2}=\pm0.1\sqrt{P_k}\)
after \(f\) \(y_0=0,\ y_{1,2}=0.01\,P_k\)
mean of \(y\) \(\bar{y}=P_k\)
+

Weights

mean \(w_0=-99,\ w_{1,2}=50\)
covariance \(w_0^c=-98.01+\beta\)

The tug of war that builds the new variance \(P_{k+1}\)

\(\beta=+2\)
outer: \(\sum_{i=1}^{2}w_i\,(y_i-\bar{y})^2\) = +98.01 \(P_k^2\)
center: \(w_0^c\,(y_0-\bar{y})^2\) = −96.01 \(P_k^2\)
\(P_{k+1}=+2.00\,P_k^2>0\) ✓ valid variance
\(\beta=-2\)
outer: \(\sum_{i=1}^{2}w_i\,(y_i-\bar{y})^2\) = +98.01 \(P_k^2\)
center: \(w_0^c\,(y_0-\bar{y})^2\) = −100.01 \(P_k^2\)
\(P_{k+1}=-2.00\,P_k^2<0\) ✗ negative variance

The two sides almost cancel, so a tiny leftover decides the sign: \(\beta\) tips the balance.
