UKF Math Breakdown: When Roots Go Negative

How bad hyperparameters (\(\alpha, \beta, \kappa\)) break the 2D Cholesky decomposition \(\mathbf{L}\)

1. The Sigma Spread & The Trap

Let \(\gamma = \sqrt{n+\lambda}\). Points extract columns of \(\mathbf{L}\):

\(\displaystyle \mathbf{x}_i = \mathbf{\hat{x}} \pm \left ( \sqrt{(n+\lambda) \mathbf{P}}\right)_i = \mathbf{\hat{x}} \pm \gamma \cdot (\mathbf{L})_i \)
\(\displaystyle \lambda = \alpha^2(n+\kappa) - n \)
\(\displaystyle w_0^c = \frac{\lambda}{n+\lambda} + (1 - \alpha^2 + \beta) \)
⚠️ The Chain Reaction
  1. Small \(\alpha\) (e.g., \(10^{-3}\)) \(\implies \lambda \approx -n\)
  2. Massive Negative Weight:
    \(\displaystyle w_0^c \approx \frac{(-n)}{\varepsilon} + (1-\varepsilon^2 + \beta) = -M \ll 0 \)
    where \( 0<\varepsilon\ll 1 \) and \( 1 \ll M < +\infty \)
  3. Corrupted Covariance:
    \(\displaystyle \mathbf{P} = \color{red}{w_0^c} (\mathbf{y}_0 - \mathbf{\hat{x}})(\dots)^T + \sum_{i=1}^{2n} w_i (\dots) \)
  4. Result: \(\mathbf{P}\) loses Positive Definiteness.
→

2. Explicit 2D Matrix Root (\(\mathbf{L}\))

Next iteration needs \(\mathbf{L}\) where \(\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^T\):

\(\displaystyle \mathbf{P} = \begin{bmatrix} p_{11} & p_{12} \\ p_{12} & p_{22} \end{bmatrix} \)

The standard 2D decomposition yields:

\(\displaystyle \mathbf{L} = \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{p_{11}} & 0 \\ \frac{p_{12}}{\sqrt{p_{11}}} & \sqrt{p_{22} - \frac{p_{12}^2}{p_{11}}} \end{bmatrix} \)
→

3. The Breaking Points

The corrupted \(\mathbf{P}\) makes \(\mathbf{L}\) impossible to compute:

Crash 1: Negative Variance

\(\displaystyle L_{11} = \sqrt{\mathbf{p}_{11}} \)

Fails if \(p_{11} < 0\).

Crash 2: Invalid Determinant

\(\displaystyle L_{22} = \sqrt{\mathbf{p}_{22} - \frac{p_{12}^2}{p_{11}}} = \sqrt{\frac{\det(\mathbf{P})}{p_{11}}} \)

Fails if \(\det(\mathbf{P}) < 0\).
