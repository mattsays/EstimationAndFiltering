The Tug of War Inside \(\mathbf{P}_{k+1}\)
How a small \(\alpha\) and a nonlinear \(\mathbf{f}\) put the sign of the variance up for grabs
1. Small \(\alpha\) ⇒ huge weights
From \(\lambda=\alpha^2(n+\kappa)-n\), define \(\varepsilon:=n+\lambda=\alpha^2(n+\kappa)\):
\(\displaystyle w_i=\frac{1}{2\varepsilon},\quad w_0=\frac{\lambda}{n+\lambda}=1-\frac{n}{\varepsilon}\)
\(\displaystyle w_0^c=w_0+1-\alpha^2+\beta\approx-\frac{n}{\varepsilon}\)
As \(\alpha\to0\): \(w_i\to+\infty\), \(w_0^c\to-\infty\). E.g. \(n=2,\ \kappa=0,\ \alpha=10^{-3}\): \(w_i=2.5\cdot10^5\), \(w_0^c\approx-10^6\).
→
2. Nonlinear \(\mathbf{f}\) ⇒ \(\mathbf{e}_0\neq\mathbf{0}\)
Mean and deviations of the images \(\mathbf{y}_i=\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}_i)\):
\(\displaystyle \bar{\mathbf{y}}=w_0\mathbf{y}_0+\sum_{i=1}^{2n}w_i\mathbf{y}_i\)
\(\displaystyle \mathbf{e}_i=\mathbf{y}_i-\bar{\mathbf{y}}\)
Linear \(\mathbf{f}\): \(\bar{\mathbf{y}}=\mathbf{y}_0\), so \(\mathbf{e}_0=\mathbf{0}\) (weights sum to 1, outer points are symmetric). Nonlinear \(\mathbf{f}\): \(\mathbf{e}_0\neq\mathbf{0}\).
→
3. Recombine into \(\mathbf{P}_{k+1}\)
\(\displaystyle \mathbf{P}_{k+1} = \color{#dc2626}{\overbrace{w_0^c\,\mathbf{e}_0\mathbf{e}_0^T}^{\text{negative}}} + \color{#16a34a}{\overbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{2n}w_i\,\mathbf{e}_i\mathbf{e}_i^T}^{\text{positive}}}\)
The center point enters with a negative weight, meaning the red term is subtracted from the green term.
(Process noise \(\mathbf{Q}\) omitted.)
Variance along a direction \(\mathbf{v}\)
Since \(\mathbf{v}^T(\mathbf{e}\mathbf{e}^T)\mathbf{v}=(\mathbf{v}^T\mathbf{e})^2\):
\(\displaystyle \mathbf{v}^T\mathbf{P}_{k+1}\mathbf{v} = \color{#dc2626}{\overbrace{w_0^c\,s_0^2}^{\text{negative}}} + \color{#16a34a}{\overbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{2n}w_i\,s_i^2}^{\text{positive}}}\)
with \(s_i=\mathbf{v}^T\mathbf{e}_i\). \(\mathbf{P}_{k+1}\) is PD iff this is \(>0\) for every \(\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{0}\).
The tug of war
Outer points are only \(\gamma=\sqrt{\varepsilon}\) from the center, so \(\mathbf{e}_i\approx\mathbf{e}_0\) and the two teams pull almost equally:
1 center point
\(w_0^c s_0^2\approx-\dfrac{n}{\varepsilon}s_0^2\)
\(\mathbf{v}^T\mathbf{P}\mathbf{v}<0\) \(\mathbf{v}^T\mathbf{P}\mathbf{v}>0\)
\(2n\) outer points
\(\sum w_i s_i^2\approx+\dfrac{n}{\varepsilon}s_0^2\)
Both are huge (\(\propto1/\varepsilon\)). What remains is a tiny difference of the two: nothing forces it to be positive.
