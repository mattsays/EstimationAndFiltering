What is \(w_0^c\)?

Encoding missing shape information into the covariance \(\mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}\)

1. The Truncation Problem

Non-linear functions distort the distribution. The basic algorithm matches moments perfectly up to the 3rd, but introduces a truncation error at the 4th moment (kurtosis).

\(\displaystyle \begin{array}{ccc} \textbf{Prior} & \xrightarrow{\ f_k(\mathbf{x})\ } & \textbf{Distorted} \\ (\text{Skew} = 0) & \longrightarrow & \text{Exact Match} \\ (\text{Kurtosis} = 3) & \longrightarrow & \text{Truncation Error} \end{array} \)

2. The Solution

We fix this by tuning only the central weight \(w_0^c\). This safely resizes the covariance spread without shifting the mean \(\mathbf{\hat{x}}\).

3. Fixing the Taylor Error

Adding a compensation term to the central weight forces the Unscented Transform to match the exact Taylor Series at the 4th order:

\(\displaystyle \mathbf{P}_{\text{UT}}^{\text{(uncorrected)}} = \sum_{i=0}^{2n} w_i (\cdots)(\cdots)^T = [\text{2nd}] + [\text{3rd}] + {\color{red}[\text{Wrong 4th}]} + \cdots \)
\(\displaystyle \mathbf{P}_{\text{UT}}^{\text{(corrected)}} = \sum_{i=1}^{2n} w_i (\cdots)(\cdots)^T + {\color{blue}w_0^c} (\cdots)(\cdots)^T = [\text{2nd}] + [\text{3rd}] + {\color{green}[\text{Correct 4th}]} + \cdots \)
\(\displaystyle w_0^c = w_0 + (1 - \alpha^2 + \beta) \)

For a Gaussian prior, setting \(\beta = 2\) neutralizes the 4th-order error. Higher odd moments remain zero natively due to symmetry.

Geometric Intuition

The \(\beta\) parameter safely inflates the covariance \(\mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}\) to capture the heavier tails (4th moment), anchoring the mean \(\mathbf{\hat{x}}\) in its exact position.
