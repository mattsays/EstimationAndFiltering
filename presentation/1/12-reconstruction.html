Sigma Points \(\to\) Gaussian Distribution

From discrete transformed points \(\mathbf{y}_i\) back to continuous moments \(\mathbf{\hat{x}}_{k+1|k}\) and \(\mathbf{P}_{k+1|k}\)

Mapped Cloud

\(2n+1\) discrete points floating in distorted space:

\(\displaystyle \mathbf{y}_i = f_k(\mathbf{x}_i), \quad i=0,\dots,2n \)
→

Center of Mass \(=\) Mean (\(\mathbf{\hat{x}}\))

Weighted average yields the predicted mean state:

\(\displaystyle \mathbf{\hat{x}}_{k+1|k} = \sum_{i=0}^{2n} w_i \mathbf{y}_i \)

Uses base mass (mean) weights \(w_i\).

Moment of Inertia \(=\) Covariance (\(\mathbf{P}\))

Reconstructs the Gaussian covariance shape:

\(\displaystyle \mathbf{P}_{k+1|k} = \sum_{i=1}^{2n} w_i (\mathbf{y}_i - \mathbf{\hat{x}})(\mathbf{y}_i - \mathbf{\hat{x}})^T \)
\(\displaystyle + w_0^c (\mathbf{y}_0 - \mathbf{\hat{x}})(\mathbf{y}_0 - \mathbf{\hat{x}})^T \)

The result when plotting the two moments:

Wait... Why is \(w_0^c\) added to the formula?
  • Outer points (\(i \ge 1\)): use base weight \(w_i\)
  • Central point (\(i = 0\)): uses different weight \(w_0^c\)
Why does the central point \(\mathbf{y}_0\) carry a special statistical significance that requires this extra term?
