Do We Need Higher-Degree Cubature Rules?
The third-degree spherical-radial rule is enough
Fig. 4.2: mean and covariance of a generic \(\mathbf{z}_c=[r\cos\theta,\ r\sin\theta]^T\)
Little accuracy gain
Polar → Cartesian test (\(\bar r=80\), \(\bar\theta=0.61\)), Monte Carlo with 5000 samples.
Both rules hit the true mean; the 3rd-degree covariance is close to the 5th-degree one, and both deviate slightly from the truth.
Cost and stability
3rd degree: \(2n\) points, all weights equal and positive (linear in \(n\)).
5th degree: of order \(n^2\) points, some with negative weights, causing numerical instability and the curse of dimensionality.
