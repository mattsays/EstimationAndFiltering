Spherical-Radial Decomposition
How a Gaussian-weighted integral splits into a radial and a spherical one
1. Weighted integral
Nonlinear function times a Gaussian weight over \(\mathbb{R}^n\):
\(\displaystyle I(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^n} f(x)\,e^{-x^{T}x}\,dx\)
→
2. Change of variables \(x=ry\)
With \(r\ge 0\) and \(y\) on the unit sphere \(U_n\): \(x^Tx=r^2\) and \(dx=r^{n-1}\,dr\,d\sigma(y)\).
\(\displaystyle I(f)=\int_0^\infty\!\!\int_{U_n} f(ry)\,r^{n-1}e^{-r^2}\,d\sigma(y)\,dr\)
↓
3. Spherical integral
Inner part, unit weight \(w(y)=1\):
\(\displaystyle S(r)=\int_{U_n} f(ry)\,d\sigma(y)\)
&
4. Radial integral
Outer part, weight \(r^{n-1}e^{-r^2}\):
\(\displaystyle \int_0^\infty S(r)\,r^{n-1}e^{-r^2}\,dr\)
→
5. Decoupled rules
Each integral gets its own rule: a spherical cubature rule for \(S(r)\) and Gaussian quadrature for the radial part.
