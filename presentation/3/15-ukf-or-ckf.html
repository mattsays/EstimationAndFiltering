UKF or CKF?

Why keep UKF? Tunable sampling can improve accuracy on a specific problem.

UKF: flexibility

2n + 1 points in the standard scaled rule.

λ controls point spread and mean weights; c₀ corrects the central covariance weight.

Tuning can reduce higher-order approximation errors and outperform the fixed CKF rule on some nonlinear problems.

CKF: a fixed rule

2n points, each weight = 1/(2n).

Fixed symmetric pairs; positive equal weights.

A simpler sampling choice with no spread tuning. Positive weights alone do not guarantee the best accuracy.

UKF offers flexibility. CKF offers a fixed rule.

Strong nonlinearity can make tuning matter; it does not guarantee a UKF advantage.

Why can they look similar?

Both transform representative points through the nonlinear model.

With λ = 0, c₀ = 0, the standard UKF has the same effective points and weights as CKF. Its central point has zero weight in both mean and covariance. Our benchmark uses λ = 1 and c₀ = 2.
